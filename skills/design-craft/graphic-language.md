# Graphic language: imagery, icons, shapes, motion

The logo, type, and color are the brand's letters. The **graphic language** is its handwriting: the shapes, imagery, and motion that make unlabeled work recognizable. Keep it to **three motifs at most**, each traceable to the brand's idea.

## Derive it

Take three things from the chosen route and the logo: a **shape language** (round, angular, organic), a **line quality** (stroke weight, corner radius), and one **recurring device** (a crop, a pattern, a frame, a color block). Write each as a rule the next designer can apply without asking.

## Imagery

- Photography: subject, light (soft or hard, warm or cool), color grade (tied to the palette), crop (tight or airy), and composition (centered, off-axis). Name one real example per rule.
- Illustration: line weight, palette limited to brand tokens, level of detail, how people are drawn.
- Real beats stock: photograph the real product, place, and people when possible. Stock handshakes and generic skylines say nothing about this brand.

## Icons

Set one grid (24 px is the usual), one stroke weight (1.5 or 2 px), one cap and corner style, and one fill rule (outline or solid). Mixing sets breaks the brand faster than any color mistake.

## Patterns and shapes

Build them from the logo's geometry or the brand's subject, repeat at a scale where the pattern reads as texture, and place text only over calm regions of it.

## Motion (screen only)

Choose one easing curve and a short duration range (150 to 300 ms for interface feedback), then reuse them. Motion explains change; it does not decorate.

## Honest content

Use real numbers, real testimonials, and real photos. A figure or quote the user did not supply is a placeholder labeled as one ("metric to confirm"), never an invented claim.
