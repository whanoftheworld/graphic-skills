# Layout, hierarchy, and composition

## Read order

Decide the three reads before placing anything: what the eye hits **first**, **second**, **third**. Build them with these levers, strongest first:

- **Size**: jump at least 1.5× between levels. Smaller jumps read as a mistake, not a hierarchy.
- **Contrast and color**: the first read gets the highest contrast on the surface, and the brand's accent belongs to it alone.
- **Weight and position**: heavier and higher reads earlier; left-top is the default start in left-to-right scripts.
- **Space**: isolation reads as importance. Surround the first read with more room than anything else.

Two levels of emphasis on one surface compete. A fourth level means the message has more than one idea; cut or split the graphic.

## Grid and spacing

- Choose a column grid (4 for phone, 8 or 12 for wide) and hold its margins and gutters on every surface of the brand.
- Spacing comes from one scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 (a 4/8 base, a convention rather than a law). Name steps as tokens.
- **Proximity**: the space between groups is at least twice the space within a group. This single rule fixes most amateur layouts.
- **Alignment**: pick a few strong edges and hold them. Left-align body text; center only short, symmetrical statements of three lines or fewer.
- **Margins**: keep content at least 5 to 8% of the short side away from the trim or canvas edge. Cramped margins read cheap faster than any other flaw.

## Gestalt, as a checklist

Proximity groups things that sit close. Similarity groups things that look alike. Continuity makes the eye follow a line. Closure lets a viewer complete a shape. Figure and ground decide what advances and what recedes. When a layout confuses, one of these is working against the intent.

## Composition by surface

- **Social feed**: a graphic is judged at thumbnail size in under three seconds. One focal point, text large enough to read at 200 px wide.
- **Text over photography**: add a scrim or choose a quiet region, then verify contrast on the busiest pixels under the text, not the average.
- **Print**: reserve 3 mm bleed and keep text 5 mm inside the trim; set safe areas from the printer's spec, not memory.
- **Platform sizes and safe zones change.** Confirm the current spec before fixing a canvas size.

## Checks before handing over

1. **Squint test**: blur or shrink the graphic; the three reads survive in order.
2. **Grayscale test**: remove color; hierarchy and legibility survive.
3. **Thumbnail test**: view at 100 to 200 px wide; the message is still the first thing seen.
4. **Remove test**: delete each element in turn; if nothing is lost, it stays deleted.
