# Design grilling

Interview the human relentlessly until the brand or brief is clear. Map the subject as a **design tree**: every decision branches into the decisions that hang off it.

Work the tree in **rounds**. The **frontier** is every decision whose prerequisites are settled. Ask the frontier in one round, at most five questions, numbered, each with your recommended answer. Wait for the answers, recompute the frontier, ask the next round. A question that depends on another open question in the same round belongs to a later round.

```
❓ **Q1** - **<title>**: <question, with a one-line gloss for any design term>

➡️ <your recommended answer and why, in one line>
```

## Facts are yours, decisions are theirs

Competitors, font availability, platform sizes, and anything in their files you look up yourself. Never ask the human for what a search or a file read can settle. Put each decision to them and wait.

## Show, don't tell

Taste is hard to say and easy to point at. When a question stalls, make the choice visible: two or three contrasting options as swatches, type specimens, or small mocks rendered in HTML, then ask "which feels more like you?". Contrast beats description: pick options that differ on one axis at a time.

## Evidence over adjectives

When the human names or shows something they like or dislike, ask what exactly pulls them: shape, weight, spacing, color, mood, one attribute. Record the attribute with the item. "I like Nike" is trivia; "I like how one bold shape carries the whole brand" is a design requirement.

## Plain language

No design jargon without a one-line gloss. Reply in the user's language. "I don't know" is an answer: turn it into a visible probe, or defer the decision with a default and a reason.

## Done

The session is done when the frontier is empty: every branch is decided, deferred with a reason, or not applicable, and nothing was silently assumed. Write each round into the brand memory as it closes, not at the end. Read the summary back in plain words and get an explicit yes before acting on it.
