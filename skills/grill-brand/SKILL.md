# Grill brand

Shape the brand by interview, the way a studio listens before it draws. Load the `brand-context` and `design-grilling` skills (the Skill tool, or read their SKILL.md), and run the interview they define over the **brand tree** below. Reply in the user's language and write every closed round into the brand memory.

If `BRAND.md` exists, read it first and start the tree at its first `open` or `observed` node; never re-ask what is `decided`.

## The brand tree

Each node lists what it decides. A node opens when the nodes above it are settled.

**A. Ground** (opens first; most of it can be asked together)

- **A1 Purpose**: what the business sells or does, for whom, and why it matters, in one sentence.
- **A2 Audience**: one real person (give them a name and a day), then the wider segment, and what they compare this brand with.
- **A3 Channels**: where graphics will live, in priority order (LINE OA, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, marketplace listings, print, packaging, signage, web). This sets formats and sizes for everything later.
- **A4 Category**: three to five competitors or neighbours. You research them and bring back the category's visual clichés; the human reacts.
- **A5 Onlyness** (after A1, A2, A4): finish "We are the only ___ that ___ for ___ because ___" ([Neumeier](https://danmall.com/posts/onlyness/)). Swap-test it: put a competitor's name in; if it still fits, the statement is not done.

**B. Character** (needs A)

- **B1 Personality spectrum**: place the brand 1 to 5 on playful ↔ serious, classic ↔ modern, warm ↔ cool, loud ↔ quiet, accessible ↔ premium, handmade ↔ engineered. Offer your reading from A as the recommended answer.
- **B2 Archetype**: a primary and a secondary from the twelve commonly used (Innocent, Sage, Explorer, Outlaw, Magician, Hero, Lover, Jester, Everyman, Caregiver, Ruler, Creator), as a lens on the brand, never a label to print.
- **B3 Say and never**: three "___ but never ___" pairs ("confident but never arrogant").
- **B4 Voice**: formality, and two sample lines in the brand's own language.

**C. Taste evidence** (needs B; make the human bring things)

- **C1 Logos** they love and hate, 3 to 5 each, as images or names, each with the one attribute behind the reaction.
- **C2 Fonts** they love and hate: a name, a screenshot, or "the text on that sign", with the attribute. You read the anatomy and say what it points to.
- **C3 Colors and moods**: images, products, places, or packaging that feel right.
- **C4 Borrowed feeling**: brands outside the category they would like to feel like, and brands they must not resemble.
- **C5 Constraints**: assets that must stay, colors or symbols to avoid, cultural or religious considerations, font budget (free OFL only, or paid), print needs (one-color stamps, embroidery), and languages in the logo.

**D. Identity calls** (needs A to C; settles whether, not how)

- **D1 Symbol or wordmark**: answer Haviv's reason test from `logo.md`; the shape itself comes later in `/logo-lab`.
- **D2 Name status**: final, or still moving (naming is outside this skill; flag it).

## Rules for this tree

- Taste questions cannot take a recommended answer from thin air. Derive your pick from A and B, state the derivation, and render a small A/B probe when the human stalls.
- Every image the human sends gets read in front of them: say what you see in terms of attributes, then ask whether that is what they meant.
- Reference brands are material, not templates. Take the attribute, never the look.

## Close

The interview is done when every node is `decided`, `deferred` with a reason, or not applicable, and the **onlyness statement passes its swap-test**. Write the one-page brand summary (purpose, onlyness, audience, personality, taste profile in ten lines) into the brand memory, read it back, and get an explicit yes.

Then tell the human the next command: `/design-routes`.
